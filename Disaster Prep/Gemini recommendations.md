Okay, that's a smart approach – a lightweight "Go-Bag" for immediate grab-and-run, and a more comprehensive "Stash-Bag" for longer-term survival, positioned for quick loading into a vehicle.
Here are the reorganized lists based on your previous request, keeping in mind the current context in Mashhad.
1. The "Grab-and-Run" Go-Bag (Lightweight & Immediate Flight)
This bag should be light enough for anyone in your family to carry for an extended period if necessary. Think absolute essentials for 24-48 hours.
Bag Type: A comfortable, durable backpack (school backpack, day pack, or small hiking pack).
Weight Priority: Keep it as light as possible. Every gram counts.
Food & Water (Minimal Survival Rations)
 * Water: 1-2 small (500ml-1L) bottles of water per person.
 * Water Purification: Small bottle of water purification tablets (enough for several liters).
 * Food: 2-3 high-energy, non-perishable bars (e.g., energy bars, granola bars) per person. Something you can eat on the go without preparation.
Sheltering & Protection (Basic)
 * Emergency Blanket (Mylar): One per person. Lightweight, compact, and provides warmth.
 * Small Poncho/Rain Cover: One per person. For rain protection.
Clothing (Essential Layers)
 * Extra Socks: 1-2 pairs of wool or synthetic socks per person (crucial for foot health when walking).
 * Lightweight Outer Layer: A light jacket or long-sleeved shirt if not already wearing one.
Tools & Miscellaneous (Critical)
 * Flashlight: Small LED flashlight (with extra batteries).
 * Whistle: For signaling help.
 * Basic Multi-tool or Small Knife:
 * Power Bank: Small, fully charged power bank for phone (check compatibility with your phone cable!).
 * Important Documents:
   * Physical Copies: Photo ID (national ID, passport), driver's license. Keep these in a small, waterproof baggie.
   * Emergency Contacts: Written list of essential phone numbers (family, friends, embassy if applicable) in case your phone dies.
 * Cash: Small amount of local currency in small denominations.
 * Basic First-Aid Kit:
   * Band-aids, antiseptic wipes, small roll of medical tape.
   * Pain relievers (e.g., paracetamol/acetaminophen).
   * Any essential prescription medications (1-day supply, clearly labeled, if it's life-critical and can't be easily replaced).
Communication & Navigation (Immediate)
 * Charged Phone: (You'll likely be carrying this anyway, but ensure it's fully charged).
 * Small Paper Map: Of Mashhad and immediate surrounding areas.
2. The "Stash-Bag" / Car Storage (Comprehensive for 4-5 Days)
This larger collection of items will be stored securely and ready to load into your car if you need to evacuate for an extended period. This covers the bulk of the original "Go-Bag" list.
Bag Type: Large durable duffel bags, sturdy plastic bins, or larger hiking backpacks. Keep it organized by category.
Food & Water (Extended Supply)
 * Water: At least 3 gallons (approx. 12 liters) of bottled water per person.
 * Water Purification:
   * Larger bottle of purification tablets.
   * Portable water filter (e.g., Sawyer Mini, Lifestraw).
   * Small bottle of unscented household bleach (for water purification – clearly labeled).
 * Food (3-5 Days Non-Perishable):
   * Canned goods (fruits, vegetables, meats – with a manual can opener).
   * Larger packs of energy bars, granola bars.
   * Dried fruit, nuts, jerky.
   * Peanut butter or nut butter.
   * Crackers.
   * Instant oatmeal, instant noodles.
   * Comfort food items (small candies, chocolate).
   * Consider specific dietary needs (baby formula, pet food, etc.).
Sheltering & Protection (More Robust)
 * Tarp: Medium-sized durable tarp (for ground cover or makeshift shelter).
 * Duct Tape: Roll of heavy-duty duct tape.
 * Heavy-Duty Garbage Bags: Multiple large bags (for waste, rain collection, impromptu shelter).
 * Plastic Sheeting: Roll of medium-gauge plastic sheeting (for sealing a room, making shelter).
 * Rope/Paracord: 50-100 feet.
Clothing (Full Set of Layers)
 * Sturdy Walking Shoes/Hiking Boots: If not worn.
 * Multiple Sets of Clothes:
   * Long-sleeved shirts, long pants (durable fabrics).
   * Extra underwear.
   * Warm sweater or fleece jacket.
   * Rain jacket or waterproof poncho.
   * Hat (for sun/cold).
   * More wool/synthetic socks.
 * Gloves: Work gloves or multi-purpose gloves.
Tools & Miscellaneous (Comprehensive)
 * Flashlights: Multiple, different sizes (headlamp, larger lantern) with plenty of extra batteries.
 * Battery-powered or Hand-Crank Radio: With extra batteries.
 * Wrench/Pliers: To turn off utilities.
 * Matches (waterproof container) & Lighter.
 * Duct Tape.
 * Pen and Paper.
 * Small shovel/trowel: For sanitation.
 * Books/Small Games: For morale and passing time, especially if you have children.
Health & Hygiene (Extended)
 * Comprehensive First-Aid Kit:
   * Assorted bandages, gauze pads, medical tape.
   * Antiseptic wipes, alcohol swabs.
   * Hydrogen peroxide.
   * Burn cream, hydrocortisone cream.
   * Pain relievers, anti-diarrhea medication, antacids, antihistamines.
   * Tweezers, small scissors.
   * All Prescription Medications: At least a 7-day supply (ideally more), clearly labeled with dosage and purpose.
   * Personal Hygiene Items: Toilet paper, moist towelettes, hand sanitizer (large bottle), feminine hygiene products, soap, small towel.
   * Dust masks (N95 if possible).
   * Eyeglasses/contact lens supplies.
 * Sunscreen and Insect Repellent.
Communication & Navigation (Longer-Term)
 * Additional Power Banks: Larger capacity, or solar charger if feasible.
 * Copies of Important Documents (Waterproof):
   * All documents from "Go-Bag" + birth certificates, marriage certificates, insurance policies, bank records, medical records (including medication lists, allergies, doctor contacts). Store these securely.
 * Local and Regional Maps: Paper maps (more detailed than "Go-Bag" map).
 * Compass.
Specific to Iran/Mashhad Context
 * VPN Apps Ready: Ensure ExpressVPN, NordVPN, Surfshark are downloaded, updated, and configured with obfuscation/OpenVPN (TCP) settings before you need them.
 * Tor Browser with Bridges: Downloaded and ready to use.
 * Psiphon/Shadowsocks/V2Ray Clients: If you can safely acquire these from trusted sources, have them ready (e.g., on a USB stick or easily accessible if you get a brief connection). This would be for the "Stash-Bag" as a secondary communication method.
 * Fuel/Car Preparedness: Keep your car's fuel tank at least half full. Check tire pressure, fluid levels. A basic tool kit for your car.
Placement & Organization:
 * Go-Bag: Easily accessible by the door, in a closet, or under a bed. Each family member should have one or know where the primary one is.
 * Stash-Bag/Car Items: In a garage, storeroom, or directly in your car's trunk, covered if visible. Organize into smaller, labeled bags or bins within the larger "stash" to make it easy to find items.
By having these two levels of preparedness, you'll be ready for both immediate, unforeseen emergencies and more prolonged, planned (or forced) evacuations. Stay safe.
